# Chat bot

A bot that sits in a Douyin live room as an ordinary viewer's account and talks in the chat, answered by Claude. What it does there:

- **Answers whoever @s it**: questions about the stream, the songs, the streamer, or anything else (with web search).
- **Joins in without an @** when people talk to or about it (`/自动回复阈值 0.6`; off by default).
- **Answers questions about the room**, such as 「主播会唱晴天吗」 or 「几点开播」, from the room's own files, like its song list and streaming times (tools the room switches on in its `room_config.json`). Viewers just ask in the chat.
- **Names the songs the streamer sings** (`/识歌`, or every song with `/自动识歌 1`) and says something short about each.
- **Thanks big givers** with a message about their gift (`/自动谢礼 499`), and **answers questions about the puzzle on screen** (`ask_about_puzzle`, `/解题`).
- **Likes, lights the 灯牌 and sends gifts** when the streamer or an admin asks: likes the room (`/点赞`), sends today's 粉丝团灯牌 (`/灯牌`, 2 diamonds), and sends a 小心心 (`/小心心`, 1 diamond, at most one every 4 minutes), the only gift it sends: asked for another, it says it can only send a 小心心. With `/开心送心 1` it also sends the streamer one of its own accord when anyone makes it truly happy (being asked for one doesn't count), saying nothing about it.

To use it:

1. Start, in this order: the speech service (`uv run --directory asr transcribe.py --serve 8758`, from `chat-bot/`), the bot for a room once the service is ready: `pnpm start --room <房间号> --bot-name <its nickname>`, and last room-feed, logged in as the bot's account (more under [Start](AGENTS.md#start)). The bot never starts the speech service itself.
2. In the room, an admin (the streamer, or anyone in `config.json`) turns features on and off by asking the bot in words or with a `/` command (see Tools and `/` commands below; every command is in [`docs/commands.md`](docs/commands.md)).
3. To fit it to one room, put that room's details in its `data/<房间号>/` folder, with no code changes: who the streamer is (`memory.md`, which the bot also adds to itself), a song list, which tools it has on and their data (a song list, the streamer's hours), a message for when she sings. How is in [`docs/room-setup.md`](docs/room-setup.md).

## How it works

`chat-bot/` reads a room's chat from room-feed and keeps the last 100 messages in memory. When someone @s the bot's account, it fills [`chat-bot/prompts/prompt.md`](prompts/prompt.md) with that message, the chat before it and what the streamer said lately, runs `claude -p` (with a screenshot of the live video if `--screenshot` is on; web search and fetch plus read-only access to the chat record (WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Glob, Grep; `--tools`), no MCP servers, no saved session, since chat is untrusted), and posts `@asker reply` back to the room through room-feed's `POST /api/rooms/:roomId/chat`. Everything the bot says goes out at least 10 seconds apart, while Claude goes on to the next question. Claude answers one question at a time; when it takes the next, everything else that person said while waiting (a question, then 「我发文字你看」) comes with it as one request, answered once (`answering together {asker, questions}` in the log); other people's questions stay in the queue, in order. Messages wait in three queues, each in order (see "What goes out first" below).

- **What the streamer says**: `chat-bot/asr/transcribe.py` pulls the stream's audio with ffmpeg (the URL comes from room-feed's `GET /api/rooms/:roomId/stream`), finds sentences with fsmn-vad, tells each one's language with FireRedLID, transcribes it with [FireRedASR2-AED](https://github.com/FireRedTeam/FireRedASR2S) or, for Japanese and Korean, SenseVoice Small, and scores each for speech and music with AST (an AudioSet classifier), on Apple's GPU or CUDA. FireRedASR2-AED was picked over SenseVoice Small for making fewer mistakes with a song playing under the speech; it takes about a quarter of a sentence's length to transcribe it on an Apple GPU (SenseVoice took a hundredth), but over its length on the CPU, which then falls behind the stream. It only knows Mandarin, Chinese dialects and English, though: a Japanese song came out as romaji, `<unk>`s or loops like `HELLO HELLO …`, so those lines go to SenseVoice instead. Language ID is unreliable on short sentences, so one under 2 s skips it and goes to FireRedASR2-AED as Mandarin (`lang` empty), and only a sentence FireRedLID is at least 0.8 sure is Japanese or Korean goes to SenseVoice. FireRedLID adds about 0.15–0.2 s a sentence on an Apple GPU, and the log's `heard` shows its `lang` (`zh`, `en`, `ja`, …). FireRedASR2-AED's text has no punctuation (SenseVoice's has), and there's no emotion or background-music tag. Six or more of the same character or phrase (up to 12 characters) in a row are cut down to three. Audio stays in that process and is never written to disk; the bot keeps the text, timestamped, for 30 minutes, and gives the last 5 to the prompt as `{{transcript}}`. The worker is a service of its own (`transcribe.py --serve 8758`, on `127.0.0.1` only, `--asr-port`): you start it by hand, before the bot (see above), and the bot only connects to it, so it outlives the bot. It loads its models once, which takes about half a minute, and the bot waits for that before entering the room, so it hears the streamer from its first minute there; restarting the bot (after a code change, say) connects to the service that's still running and the models stay loaded, with the voiceprints it learned (when the bot disconnects, the service stops listening to the stream and waits for the next connection; stop it for good with Ctrl+C where it runs, or `pkill -f "transcribe.py --serve"`); when the stream ends or its URL expires, the bot asks room-feed again and gives the same worker the new URL (a `{"url": …}` line on the connection), so it's listening again within seconds and still knows the streamer's voice. A song is transcribed as if the streamer said it, so a line with a speech score under 0.2 is marked as sung (see the chat record below); the scores are also in the log (`heard`, as `speech` and `music`). AudioSet labels most songs Music rather than Singing, so the music score counts both.
- **Who's talking**: during a link-up (连线) Douyin mixes both streamers into one audio track, so the worker tells voices apart with ERes2Net voiceprints (3D-Speaker's, trained on 200k Mandarin speakers, on the GPU: about 140 ms a sentence, and 5 s more at start to compile for every sentence length; it replaced FunASR's CAM++, which split far more of a streamer's singing off as other voices). Each talked sentence joins the closest voice heard talking so far if it's at least `--same-voice` close (0.6, cosine; set on `transcribe.py`), or starts a new voice; the first voice is the streamer until another has been heard for more than twice as long. Sentences under 1.5 s are left out, as their voiceprints are too rough. Sung ones (speech under 0.2, or in Japanese or Korean, which in these rooms is nearly always lyrics and can score as speech over the music) are sorted the same way, but among the voices heard singing, apart from those heard talking, and at `--same-singer` (0.5): a singing voice doesn't match the same person's talking one (on a link-up the streamer's singing scored 0.33 against her talking, 0.28 against the guest's), and singing scores lower against the same singer than talk does. Measured on a 75-minute show where the streamer sang live for 35 minutes after half an hour of a BGM playlist with vocals, 0.5 took 4 of her 127 sung lines for another voice (long 「啊」s, a chorus over backing vocals) and none of the BGM's for hers, and kept a link-up's two singers apart; CAM++ at the 0.6 both used before took 33 of her lines for another voice. Voiceprints are taken on the whole mix: on Demucs' vocals they told her singing from the BGM no better (the numbers are in `Voiceprints` and `SAME_SINGER` in `asr/transcribe.py`). The streamer's singing voice is the first heard singing until another has sung for more than twice as long, so a guest who sings before the streamer has sung at all is taken for her at first. A line sung by another voice is labelled as that voice's, like talk (`isGuest`): `[连线](sing)` in the chat record and `（连线）（唱）` in the prompts. Naming a song someone asked for (`/识歌`, `identify_song`) still takes every sung line, whoever's voice (`isGuestTalk` leaves out only someone else's talk), while what the bot does unasked takes only the streamer's (see below): checked against the chat record in four rooms (with CAM++ at 0.6), the streamer singing in another language or style often came out as another voice (most of one streamer's Japanese songs, the chorus of another's request), besides the recordings and guests it should be. When most of a song's sung characters were another voice's (`othersShare` over 0.5), the message naming it ends with `song-others` from `wording.md` (「是连线在唱，还是在放歌？」), `identify_song` tells Claude `othersSang: true`, Codex isn't asked to write the message for a song Shazam or the lyrics search named (what it would say is about the streamer singing), and `song id asked` in the log has the share. A sung line too short for a voiceprint is the streamer's. Every sung line also goes to `data/<房间号>/sing.txt`, in the chat record's format, with its voice and how close it was to that voice: `[主播|声2|0.83](sing)`, `[连线|声3](sing)` (no closeness for a voice heard first), to see how well the singers are told apart. `asr/tests/` checks this on a recorded link-up (`uv run --directory asr pytest tests`, with `VOICE_RECORDINGS` pointing at the folder the recording is in, `chat-bot/data` by default; skipped without it: the audio isn't in git). Other voices' lines go into the chat record as `[连线]` and into the prompt marked `（连线）`; the log's `heard` shows `speaker`, `host` and `voice` (the similarity) for tuning. A sentence where both talk counts as one voice. The voices are kept for as long as the speech service runs, across bots and rooms alike (it isn't told the room), so restart it when switching rooms, or the last room's streamer stays the streamer.
- **What music is playing**: the worker keeps the stream's last 15 s of audio in memory, and when the bot asks (a `{"music_id": …}` line on the connection, for `/识别背景音乐`) looks it up by fingerprint with Shazam ([shazamio](https://github.com/shazamio/shazamio), an unofficial client: free and keyless, but it may stop working one day). The fingerprint is worked out locally and only it is sent. It finds the recording playing in the background, in any language, usually even with the streamer talking over it; it can't name a song the streamer sings themselves, which `/识歌` tries from the lyrics.
- **Singing or playing a recording**: while sentences are sung (speech under 0.2, or Japanese or Korean), the worker also looks up the vocals of the latest 15 s with Shazam itself (split from the music by Demucs `htdemucs` on the GPU, about 1 s), at most every 20 s (and again on the first sung sentence after 10 s without one), and marks a sung sentence `"played": {"title", "artist"}` when it found a recording. Only the vocals, because on the whole mix Shazam names the backing track a streamer sings over, often as the original song: on a 75-minute morning show of 海老夏二 it matched 11 of 15 windows of her singing live; on the vocals none of them, and still 3 of 5 windows of her BGM playlist (a recording's vocals are its singer's). A missed recording is taken as sung, as before. A match titled as a backing track (伴奏, Instrumental, Off Vocal, Karaoke, カラオケ, inst) is someone singing over it, not played (`BACKING_TRACK` in `asr/transcribe.py`). A played line isn't sung (`isPlayed`, `isSung`): it's `[主播](play)` in the chat record, `（放歌）` in the prompts, and goes to `sing.txt` as `[主播|声2|0.83|放 花 / 夏川りみ](play)`. The worker logs `played: …` for each lookup.
- **Where one song ends and the next begins**: besides pauses and talk (`currentSong`) and the lyrics of the song named last, two add-ons of the worker each feed a song break source of the bot (`src/core/songs/song-breaks.ts`), ranked lyrics > `shazam` > `backingGaps` > pauses; each is on unless turned off, on the worker (`--no-backing-title`, `--no-backing-gaps` on `transcribe.py`) or per room (`songBreaks` in room_config.json), and with them off the bot works as it did without them. On a 34-minute stretch of a streamer singing 8 songs over backing tracks, pauses and talk alone found 6 starts within 15 s, with 2 false breaks; with both add-ons, 8, with 2 false breaks (a one-line fragment, and a breath taken for singing before a song) (`scripts/song-break-sources.py`).
  - **Shazam's backing title** (`BackingTitles`, `src/core/songs/shazam-spans.ts`): in the cycle above, the worker looks up the whole mix first, and a sung sentence carries what it named, `"backing": {"title", "artist", "at"}`; the vocals are looked up only when the mix matched a title they weren't looked up for yet (if the mix doesn't match, the vocals won't; a backing track's title needs none), about 107 lookups an hour of singing against 95 before. A title named by two lookups in a row, after another, starts a song where the singing paused longest just before it, and the lines over one title are one song. It knew 3 of the 8 backing tracks (the others it doesn't have), which found one start pauses missed (the breath before 《年轮说》). Logs `backing: …` on the worker, `song changed {by: shazam, title, at}` on the bot.
  - **Backing gaps** (`BackingGaps`, `src/core/songs/backing-gaps.ts`): while a sentence was sung in the last minute, the worker prints, every 15 s, how loud the backing (the mix minus Demucs' vocals) was each second, `{"backing_level": {"from", "to", "db": […]}}`, splitting the audio once for this and the vocals lookup alike (about 80 ms of an Apple GPU a second of audio). The backing quiet for 3 s or more (20 dB under its median over the minute before, so the stream's level doesn't matter) between two sung lines starts a song at the second; measured throughout with no such gap, the two are one song, so talk over a backing track playing on is no break (a song sung twice over one backing was split by the talk between before). It found 7 of 8 starts. Logs `song changed {by: backingGaps, at, quietMs, line}`. A novelty curve over the backing's chroma and MFCC was tried first and found nothing: song changes scored under changes inside songs.
- **Only the streamer's own singing is acted on**: naming songs unasked (`/自动识歌`), and `on_sing` start only on lines the streamer sang herself (`isStreamerSinging`: sung, not another voice's, not played), and the song is made of her lines alone. A guest singing or a recording playing does nothing but log `not the streamer singing {by: guest|recording, text, title}`. `/识歌` and `identify_song`, asked by someone, still take every sung line.
- **Naming a song**: while the streamer is singing (a sung line in the last 15 s), Shazam is asked first, on the whole mix: she mostly sings over a backing track, which it names with its song in a second or two (in a 75-minute 海老夏二 show, 11 of 15 windows of her singing), its version tag dropped (「璀璨冒险人 (和声伴奏)」 is said as 《璀璨冒险人》; `song identified {by: shazam, shazamTitle}` in the log). Sung a cappella, nothing matched (`shazam found no song`), failed or slower than 10 s (`shazam failed`), or asked after the song: then the lyrics are searched on NetEase first (`lyrics-search/`, about a second, no Codex when it is sure of the title and a singer); Codex only when it isn't, or names no singer, with the candidates as hints. Claude may also call `identify_song` / `identify_bgm` itself while answering (see Tools and `/` commands).
- **Naming the songs sung**: with `--auto-song-id` (or `/自动识歌 1`), once the streamer has been singing a while (in the last minute at least 3 of their lines sung, 70% or more of them) and the song has 40 sung characters (a song ends at a pause of over a minute, or of over 15 s with the streamer talking; once named, only where its lyrics stop matching what's sung), the bot names it as `/识歌` would and says it in the room, only when the model checked the lyrics and is sure; not sure, it asks again about the same song once twice the lyrics are in, twice at most; again every 3 minutes of singing on (after 1 minute when it's still the song it named last), never the same song twice in an hour (see [`chat-bot/docs/commands.md`](docs/commands.md#自动识歌)).
- **What's on screen**, while screenshots are on (off by default; `--screenshot` starts with them on, and admins switch them with `/截图 1` and `/截图 0` while it runs; most streams don't need it, lessons with a board do): room-feed's `GET /api/rooms/:roomId/screenshot` (the player only, as a JPEG) goes to Claude with each question, and Claude can zoom into part of it with the `zoom` tool (an MCP server, `chat-bot/src/adapters/models/zoom-mcp.ts`, cropping with ffmpeg, which must be installed).
- **Long answers**: Claude is asked to answer in one message. When it can't, the answer is split after punctuation into up to `--max-parts` messages, `@昵称 (1/3) …`. Everything the bot sends is split this way: @ answers and unprompted ones, gift thanks, command results, song names (those to nobody in particular as `(1/2) …`). The @ uses the whole nickname when the message fits, and its first 3 characters when that's what it takes to fit, or when the answer is split.
- **The @ when it's plain whom it answers**: Claude's answer has `at` (`MENTION_ANSWER_SCHEMA`): `false` when it's obvious whom it's replying to (they just spoke, nobody cut in, a back-and-forth), and the reply goes without the `@昵称`, split as `(1/2) …` if long; `true` in a busy chat. The streamer is never @ed, whatever Claude says, in an answer to her or a message for her in `others` (`streamerId`, `src/app/replies/mention-answerer.ts`); anyone else in `others` always is.
- **Chat record**: everything the streamer says, every chat message and every gift goes into `data/<房间号>/chat/<ms>.txt`, one record per line, 100 lines per file, named by its first record's time: `2026-10-16 16:47:22+08:00 [雅#7664182420958889017](gift) 送出 人气票×1（1钻）` (the time in the room's `timeZone`, with its offset; lines without one were written before, in the machine's time zone); a chat's sender also carries what they wear after `|`, as room-feed sends it: `V` or `V年` (直播间会员, monthly or yearly), `灯牌16` (fan club level) and `财富36` (wealth level), as in `[雅#7664182420958889017|V年|灯牌16|财富36](chat)`, or `[主播](speech) …` for the streamer, `[连线](speech) …` for whoever they're linked up with, `[主播](sing) …` when sung (a speech score under 0.2; the prompt's transcript marks those lines `（唱）`), and `[主播](song) 《璀璨冒险人》唱完了，下一首从 10:48:11 开始（歌词对不上了）` when one of the streamer's songs ended: known once the next one has started (pauses and talk, or a song break source: 歌词对不上了, 伴奏换了一首, 伴奏停过), so it's written then, with when the next one started, and the title when the bot told the room that song (`SongEnds`). Lines rather than JSON halve what Claude spends reading them. Claude gets the folder (`--add-dir`) and is told in the prompt (`{{chatDir}}`) that it's the room's chat record, to read when the recent lines in the prompt aren't enough. Its file tools aren't pre-approved, so they reach only that folder: Claude Code denies reads anywhere else when nobody is there to approve them. The record is kept across runs.
- **Admins**: `chat-bot/config.json` lists the admins' user ids (`"admins": ["7664182420958889017"]`), who they are to viewers (`"adminNames": "雅"`, for the refusal when someone else types an admin-only `/` command: 「只有主播和雅才可以」). Who the bot plays is a persona file in the room's folder that `room_config.json` names (`"persona": "personas/fairy.md"`; `{{persona}}` turns it into `你的人设是…`; none without one). A room can keep several in `data/<房间号>/personas/` and switch by naming another. A file whose `---` header says `base: fairy` changes the shared `chat-bot/prompts/personas/fairy.md` by `## 小节`, and its other header lines (`主播: 茜茜`) fill the shared persona's `{{…}}`s; see [docs/room-setup.md](docs/room-setup.md). All are read on every use, so an edit applies at once; the bot never writes them. The bot reads the admins again whenever the file changes, so they can be added or removed while it runs; an edit that doesn't parse keeps the last good list (and logs a warning). The room's streamer is an admin too: room-feed reads their user id from the room page (`GET /api/rooms/:roomId`, `meta.uid`), and the bot asks for it every 10 seconds until it's there (`streamer is an admin` in the log), then writes the streamer's nickname and user id into the room's `data/<房间号>/room_config.json` (`"streamer": { "nickname", "uid" }`). In a private room (私密直播间) Douyin hides every viewer's user id, admins' and probably the streamer's included, so admins aren't recognised there: to use an admin-only tool or command, add `"111111"` to the admins (every viewer there becomes one) and take it out again after.
- **Tools and `/` commands**: what the bot can do is a set of MCP tools (`src/mcp/`, an MCP server in the bot's process: `--mcp-port`; always on, the bot doesn't start without it): the settings (auto-reply, thanks, screenshots, …), `identify_song`, `identify_bgm`, `ask_about_puzzle`, and the declarative tools in `src/mcp/tools/` a room switches on (`"tools"` in its `room_config.json`), which hand Claude their guidance and the room's data (the song list) so it answers in the same turn. A tool's `listen` block tells auto-reply what to watch for without an @. Claude gets them in every answer, so asking in words works (an admin-only tool only in an admin's or the streamer's session); `@机器人/名字 参数` calls one directly with no model, exactly as commands did (`ToolRunner`, `src/app/tools/tool-runner.ts`; a name no tool has is answered with the commands the asker may use). Each command, who may run it and its tool are in [`docs/commands.md`](docs/commands.md).
- **Talking unprompted**: when someone talks to or about the bot without @ing it, it joins in by answering them like an @ from that person (with `prompt.md`). Every 20 seconds it asks [Jev](https://typesafe.ai) (a fast yes/no classifier; put `JEV_API_KEY` in `chat-bot/.env`) how likely each new chat and thing the streamer said is about the bot; at `--auto-reply-threshold` or more (`1` by default, so off; `/自动回复阈值 0.6` turns it on), or whatever someone the bot answered in the last minute says (it's likely a reply to it), Codex picks, from [`chat-bot/prompts/reply-pick.md`](prompts/reply-pick.md), whom to answer (the lines that still need it, at most 3, maybe none), and each is answered like an @ from that person (with their own past exchanges; the streamer's spoken lines as the streamer's, by their uid). Without Codex (`--codex ''`) it doesn't talk unprompted at all. It keeps quiet for at least `--auto-reply-interval` seconds (60) after talking; `/自动回复间隔 0` doesn't wait. Streamers often read the chat aloud: what they read is matched to the chat by sound (Smith-Waterman alignment of the pinyin, `src/core/chat/reading.ts`) and marked `（念了某人的弹幕）`, so it isn't taken for them saying it. Admins change the threshold with `/自动回复阈值 0.7` (`1` turns it off). A line Jev is surer than 0.9 is about the bot is answered at once, without Codex; Codex is shown what the tools listen for (their `listen.when`), picks only whom to answer, and a line that matched a tool reaches Claude with a hint naming it.
- **Thanking big givers**: when a viewer's gifts within 5 minutes come to more than `--thanks-threshold` diamonds (99999 by default, so off; admins change it with `/自动谢礼 100`), Claude reads that viewer's own recent messages, their past exchanges with the bot and what it remembers about them (`note.md`), the room's chat and `memory.md`, and writes one @ message to thank them from `gift-thanks.md`. The prompt keeps it conservative: no comments on looks, age, money or how much they gave, no guesses about who they are, no jokes or teasing, no comparing viewers. It waits 15 seconds first, so a combo is thanked once, for everything in it. What a viewer sends after a thanks counts afresh, and is thanked again once it passes the threshold too.
- **Douyin common knowledge**: [`chat-bot/prompts/common-knowledge.md`](prompts/common-knowledge.md) answers what viewers in any room ask about Douyin itself (灯牌 and the fan club so far), in `prompt.md` as `{{commonKnowledge}}`, under a heading from `wording.md` that tells the model not to make up what isn't written there. One `## ` section a topic; what's particular to one room goes in its `memory.md` instead. Read on every use.
- **Topics and words it never takes up**: [`chat-bot/prompts/forbidden.md`](prompts/forbidden.md) has the topics. Its `## Topics` is the rules for topics that can get a room banned or throttled (lewd talk, egging on gifts, contacts off Douyin, investing, superstition, medical advice, other platforms, …), which `prompt.md` gets as `{{forbiddenTopics}}`. The words Douyin quietly holds back a message for (`警`, `编制`: the sender's account still sees it, nobody else does) are in [`chat-bot/prompts/forbidden-words.json`](prompts/forbidden-words.json), each with what to write instead: `"酒": "8+1"`, `"B站": "小破站"`, or `""` for one `*` a character. The prompts don't name them; the program swaps them in what Claude wrote before it's cut into messages (`ReplyLimits.forbidden`, `src/core/replies/reply.ts`), so each part still fits, and the outbox swaps any left, like a nickname in an @ (`… had forbidden words, swapped`). Read on every use, so adding a word applies at once.
- **Prompts and room files**: the prompts are shared by every room: [`chat-bot/prompts/song-id.md`](prompts/song-id.md) for `/识歌`, [`chat-bot/prompts/solve.md`](prompts/solve.md) for `ask_about_puzzle`, [`chat-bot/prompts/gift-thanks.md`](prompts/gift-thanks.md) for thanking big givers, besides `prompt.md`. [`chat-bot/prompts/intro.md`](prompts/intro.md) is what the bot says about itself (who made it, what it can and can't do), as `{{intro}}` in `prompt.md`, so it answers questions about itself the same way every time. [`chat-bot/prompts/limitations.md`](prompts/limitations.md) is what it can't do and why (mishearing, link-up voices, not seeing the video, why it sometimes doesn't answer, memory, song ID, chat limits), as `{{limitations}}` in `prompt.md`, so when viewers notice something wrong it can explain in a line; it's read on every use. What's particular to a room goes in `data/<房间号>/memory.md` (who the streamer is, running jokes, what to mind), which every prompt gets as `{{memory}}` with the streamer's `note.md`; the bot adds to both itself with `update_memory`, as it does to each viewer's `<user id>/note.md` (see "What the bot remembers" in [`docs/commands.md`](docs/commands.md)). A room can still override a prompt by putting a file of the same name there with `_` for `-`: its own `song_id.md` overrides `song-id.md` (`prompt.md` has no room override). All of these are read every time they're used, so edits apply while the bot runs.
- **Seeing gifts**: every prompt's `{{history}}` shows the gifts of the last 5 minutes among the chat, by time (`澳***: （送出 比心兔兔×1，299钻）`), so when the streamer says 「谢一下这个礼物」 the bot knows which one.
- **Setting up a room**: each room's folder can hold the tools it switches on (`can_sing`, answered from its song list), files to download on entering, a message for when the streamer sings, and its own prompts; how is in [`chat-bot/docs/room-setup.md`](docs/room-setup.md).
- **Knowing its own settings**: `prompt.md` gets the room's settings as they are now (`{{settings}}`: screenshots on or off, the auto-reply threshold, the gift-thanks threshold), so a viewer asking why nobody thanked them is told that thanks are an admin setting, and a streamer asking why the bot is quiet is told how @s and auto-reply work. What the bot can do is the tools Claude has, not a list in the prompt.
- **Not repeating itself**: everything the bot sends goes into the room's `bot_logs.md`, and each @ and its answer into `<user id>/talk.txt`. Every prompt gets the bot's last 40 messages (`{{botTalk}}`), and a mention also the asker's last 10 exchanges (`{{userHistory}}`), with the instruction not to say the same things again. These are plain text rather than resumed Claude Code sessions, which would replay every earlier prompt (transcripts, chat, screenshots) and grow without end.
- **What goes out first**: the bot sends one message at a time (`--send-interval`), from three queues: replies, then system messages (command results), then what it says unprompted.

It needs [room-feed](../room-feed) running for the room and logged in as the bot's account (`pnpm room-feed login`), Claude Code's `claude`, `ffmpeg` and [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) on `PATH`. The first run downloads the speech models: FireRedASR2-AED (4.7 GB) and FireRedLID (3.5 GB) into `~/.cache/fireredasr2`, fsmn-vad and SenseVoice Small (0.9 GB) into `~/.cache/modelscope` and AST into `~/.cache/huggingface`. From `chat-bot/`, start the speech service first, and leave it running (it keeps the models loaded, so restarting the bot after a code change doesn't reload them; the bot only tells it to quit, with `{"quit": true}`, when room-feed has gone and the bot exits too):

```sh
uv run --directory asr transcribe.py --serve 8758
```

then, once it prints `[asr] serving on 127.0.0.1:8758`, the bot (it waits for room-feed, logging `room-feed error` until it's up):

```sh
pnpm start --room <房间号> --bot-name <the logged-in account's nickname> [--bot-id <its uid>]
```

then room-feed (from the repo root: `pnpm room-feed --room <房间号>`).

Without the speech service running, the bot still enters the room, without speech (it logs `no speech service on port 8758: start it first, …` once), and starts hearing the streamer within 10 s of it being started; `--no-asr` turns speech off altogether.

After a stream, `pnpm remember` with the same `--room`, `--bot-name` and `--bot-id` reads its chat into what the bot remembers: the regulars' `note.md`, the room's `memory.md` and the streamer's note (see "Reading the chat into memory" in [`docs/commands.md`](docs/commands.md)).

Options that are a room's settings (`--screenshot`, `--auto-reply-threshold`, `--auto-reply-interval`, `--thanks-threshold`, `--auto-song-id`, `--malice-watch`) only seed them: the first time a room is started, a setting its `data/<房间号>/room_config.json` doesn't have yet is filled in with the option you gave, else its default; after that the file is the source of truth, read again whenever it changes, and the admin commands write to it (fields and defaults in [`docs/room-setup.md`](docs/room-setup.md)). An option that differs from what the file has is ignored, with a `setting from room_config, ignoring option` warning.

The console shows info and up (mentions, gifts, what was heard, replies); `logs/chat-bot.log` has everything, including every chat line and prompt, as JSON lines. `--bot-id` makes @mentions match by user id rather than nickname; room-feed shows it as the `user.id` of the bot's own chat messages.

The room-feed client (`RoomFeedClient`), its event types and the port and room-number checks come from [`@dy-apps/room-feed-protocol`](../room-feed-protocol), a workspace package; the bot imports nothing from the site.
